# What `metrics.json` stores in `val_engine_rmse`, and what the ledger holds

Generated by `recompute_val.py`; do not hand-edit.

Two conventions, named explicitly, because they differ:

| convention | label | prediction | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **primary (historical)** | capped at 125 | left alone | `metrics.py` computes `rmse`/`per_engine_rmse` from the stored predictions; `BASELINE_PROTOCOL_AUDIT.md` section 0.2 says the labels are clipped and the predictions are not |
| sensitivity | capped at 125 | clipped to [0, 125] | the sprint evaluation protocol |

**The sensitivity is not a correction.** A cell whose two readings differ does not have a wrong historical value; it has a value under a different convention. 196 of 265 runs have at least one prediction above 125.

## The ledger reproduces exactly under the primary convention

`a8_engine_level.csv::val_eng` is the engine-mean with the label capped and the prediction untouched: max abs delta 5.3e-15 over 96 cells.
That is the historical convention, so the ledger is exact, not approximate. The clipped reading differs by up to 0.2172 on the same cells, and that difference is a convention change, not an error.

## Pooled is not a weighting difference

Every engine contributes exactly 5 validation windows, so pooled and engine-mean weight each engine identically at 1/E. The difference is the order of aggregation: `sqrt(mean_e MSE_e) >= mean_e sqrt(MSE_e)` by Jensen, which is also what `metrics.py::per_engine_rmse` documents. A table that reports both without saying which one selected the model is mixing two estimators, but it is not reweighting engines.
