
Two further observations bear on how the results should be read. First, the plain backbone is a strong baseline: across five folds it is only 3.3 percent behind the prior-augmented model on FD002 and 1.1 percent behind on FD004. The prior injection is therefore worth about one to three percent, and on FD004 it degrades the asymmetric score (8.98 against 11.25). Second, engine-level mixup applied to the plain backbone, which adds no parameters, reaches parity with the prior-augmented model on FD002 (+0.3 percent) and beats it on FD004 (1.7 percent, with the asymmetric score falling from 11.25 to 7.00). Two very different devices, injecting information and constraining the model, land in the same place. The natural reading is that the prior's contribution is regularisation rather than information, and that it can be obtained without a lifetime prior at all.

The screening protocol deserves a caveat of its own. Our single-fold screens use roughly fifty engines, and the run-to-run noise of the frozen recipe is about three percent, which is the same order as the effects being screened. The mixup arm looked like a 3.5 percent gain against the prior arm on two folds and settled at 0.3 percent on five. We therefore report the single-fold screens as direction-finding only, and we do not treat any of them as a result. The full matrices, not the screens, carry the conclusions.
